# PPR ego-graph expansion A/B — REJECTED for recall (2026-08-02)

## Verdict

**REJECTED** as a recall lever. PPR expansion (`ego_graph.expansion_mode:
"ppr"`) is quality-neutral in aggregate but its only cleanly-attributable
per-query effects are **losses** (Q51 −0.167 iron-clad, Q70 → 0.0, Q108
small), and it rescues none of the graph-adjacent watch-list misses
(Q121/Q122 zero in all eight runs; H063 lands at 0.0). The deployed config
stays `expansion_mode: "bfs"`.

**Recorded for the future, out of this campaign's scope**: PPR is ~15%
*faster* than BFS (3,780/3,787 ms vs 4,464/4,437 ms avg/query, replicated)
and showed tighter round-to-round variance (15 mrr flips vs 25; recall@10
spread 0.0001 vs 0.0186; 2 pool_hit flips vs 4). If a latency campaign ever
runs, PPR belongs on its candidate list alongside `single_pass` — with the
Q51/Q70 quality debit as the known cost.

This was the first-ever PPR measurement: the orchestrator bug fixed in
ADR-0020 (commit `38950a4`) previously made `expansion_mode` dead config in
production, and the benchmark's direct-`HybridSearcher` path is how it became
testable.

**Gate's primary criterion — "per-query wins on graph-adjacent misses" —
fails**: every candidate win dissolves under flapper scrutiny, while Q51
survives it as a loss.

## Interpretation

PPR ranks ego-graph neighbors by stationary visit probability instead of
BFS hop-order. That concentrates the neighbor budget on globally-central
nodes — which is why it is faster (smaller effective expansion sets reaching
the reranker) and why it hurts Q51/Q70-class queries: their golds are
low-centrality neighbors that BFS's hop-order traversal kept but PPR's
centrality-weighted budget squeezes out. The graph-adjacent stable misses
(Q121/Q122) are not expansion-selection problems at all (rrf-arithmetic /
merged-cut per `STABLE_MISS_DIAGNOSIS_20260802.md`), so no expansion-mode
change could have rescued them.

## Consequences

- `ego_graph.expansion_mode` stays `"bfs"` (deployed and default).
- Do not re-propose PPR for recall. Re-propose only in an explicit
  latency/stability campaign, with the Q51/Q70 debit priced in.
- Campaign Steps 2–4 have now all rejected; the recall frontier is
  unambiguous: merged-cut (8/17 graded golds) and the final-pool-assembly
  reserve design are the only remaining levers with any evidence behind them.
